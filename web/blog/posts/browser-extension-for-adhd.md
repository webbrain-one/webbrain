WebBrain is the browser extension for ADHD. If your browser feels like a collection of half-finished jobs, the idea is simple: visit a page, hand off a mundane task, and go to the next tab for the next job.

## AI belongs in the browser

Ad blockers made filtering unwanted content part of browsing. Crypto wallets brought signing and transactions into the browser for people who use them. We believe AI belongs alongside these tools as a core component of browsing: something you reach for right where the work happens.

You already have the page open. You already know what you want from it. Tell WebBrain what to do, with the page as context, and let it help with the reading, searching, and repetitive clicks between you and a finished task.

## Hand off the tedious part

Sometimes a small job has more steps than it deserves. Finding one detail means opening several sections. Comparing options means collecting the same information over and over. Reading a long page means hunting for the three lines that matter to you.

WebBrain gives you a way to delegate those steps while you work on something else. Start a task in one tab, move to another job, and come back to review the result. You and your browser agent can work in parallel.

The useful unit of work is small and concrete: a shortlist, a summary, a set of dates, a draft ready to review. Give each task a clear finish line so you can check it off and move on.

## A few things to hand off

Here are example requests to try on pages you already use:

- **Turn a long article into an actionable note.** “Summarize this article in five bullets and list anything I should follow up on.” Move to your writing tab while WebBrain works through the reading.
- **Narrow down a shopping list.** “Find three desks on this site under $300 that are less than 120 cm wide. Compare dimensions, price, and delivery information.” Work on your room plan, then return to review the shortlist.
- **Pull the details out of an event page.** “Find the registration deadline, ticket price, location, and what I need to bring.” Keep planning the rest of your week while the details are collected.
- **Get through repetitive research.** “Look through these course listings and make a shortlist of evening classes for beginners. Include schedules and fees where available.” Use the next tab to work on your application notes.
- **Make a support request easier to start.** “Read this return policy and draft a message asking how to return an unopened item.” Come back to a draft you can review and send yourself.

Each request specifies what to look for and what the result should contain. That makes the handoff easier to review than an open-ended “help me with this.”

## Turn open tabs into checked boxes

A simple rhythm helps: open the relevant page, describe one task, delegate it, and move to the next job. When you return, review the result and mark the task done. If WebBrain needs a detail from you, answer it and continue.

For an ADHD-oriented workflow, the appeal is having somewhere to put the next concrete action while your attention moves to another piece of work. A tab can hold a job in progress instead of being another reminder you have to keep in your head.

Start with two tasks: one for WebBrain and one for you. Let it gather the facts while you make the decision, extract the details while you write, or prepare a draft while you handle the next item on your list.

That is the browsing experience we want: AI close at hand, mundane work easier to delegate, and more to-dos reaching the checked-off stage.
